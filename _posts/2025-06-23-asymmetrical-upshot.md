This is an idea that I've been thinking about for some time now. It's a kind of _value-skewed tradeoff function_ around a behavioural mean. 

The premise: extremes in any behavior aren't  good.. But there are activities/behaviours for which the upshot is symmetric (i.e. too little of the thing is just as bad as too much of the thing), but there exist certain domains in which I believe have asymmetrical upshot (for example, confidence - I believe having too much confidence and/or too little confidence are both detrimental, but I believe that too little self-confidence is a worse problem to have than too much of it.  Society can oftentimes reward the individual who overestimates their capabilities -  but too little confidence is invisibility. **Curiosity**: Too much lead to chronic distraction, and/or alienating other's, but too little is intellectual death. Exercise**: Overtraining can cause injury, but undertraining invites slow demise.

Choice architecture also offers a kind of reverse-asymmetric upshot I feel, but it masquerades as symmetric, or even forward-asymmetric at times. We may start off by hearing, choice is good. Lots of options = Keeping doors open = combinatorial explosion of possibilities. But then later we might hear, little choice is bad, but too much choice is as bad (analysis paralysis, anxiety, grass is greener syndrome). But actually I posit that there is revers asymmetric upshot with choice. Oftentimes constraining. Our choices feeling like we have too little to choose from is actually better than having too much, despite what we've heard.

Wealth may be another loose example of asymmetrical upshot, again skewing in the green for too much, and worse for too little.  And there are other domains with this asymmetrical upshot. Drinking water is a symmetrical upshot, at either extreme death is imminent. 

I  suppose it's also contingent on the value judgement we ascribe to these concepts.
